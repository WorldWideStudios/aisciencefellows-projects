L92:To further explore the mechanism by which BMSCs exert antifibrotic effects, we investigated their impact on immune cell dynamics, particularly macrophage activation, within the fibrotic lung microenvironment. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis revealed a substantial expansion of M1-type macrophages in SiO₂-exposed lungs, especially at day 7 post-exposure (Fig.  6 A–B). GO enrichment analysis of M1 macrophage clusters indicated suppressed expression of genes associated with signal transduction, GTPase activity, and actin cytoskeletal organization (Fig.  6 C–D). Immune scoring confirmed that M1 macrophages displayed heightened activation in the SiO₂ group compared to controls (Fig.  6 E ).  In line with these findings, immunofluorescence staining and qPCR analysis of lung tissue showed that the M1 marker NOS2 and the M2 marker ARG1 were markedly upregulated in SiO₂-treated mice, whereas both single and repeated BMSC administration progressively reduced their expression (Figure S2B and S2C), further supporting that BMSCs suppress macrophage activation in vivo. L95:Previous studies have shown that activated macrophages secrete pro-fibrotic cytokines that stimulate fibroblast proliferation and migration, thereby accelerating fibrogenesis [ 14 ,  15 ]. To investigate whether ZC3H4-mediated macrophage activation influences fibroblast behavior, we collected conditioned media from THP-1-derived macrophages and applied it to GFP-labeled human pulmonary fibroblasts (GFP-HPF-a). As shown in Figs.  6 J, K, conditioned media from SiO₂-stimulated macrophages significantly enhanced fibroblast migration in a wound healing assay. Notably, this effect was abolished when ZC3H4 expression was silenced in macrophages, indicating that ZC3H4 contributes to macrophage-mediated fibroblast activation.

L97:Finally, to determine whether BMSCs therapy regulates ZC3H4 expression in vivo, we performed Western blot analysis of lung tissues from BMSCs-treated and untreated silicosis mice. BMSCs transplantation significantly reduced the elevated levels of ZC3H4 in fibrotic lung tissues. Consistently, immunofluorescence staining revealed reduced ZC3H4 expression in the lungs of BMSCs-treated mice compared to the untreated model group (Fig.  7 A and S3E). To further validate this effect at the cellular level, we examined whether BMSCs modulate ZC3H4 expression in macrophages. Macrophages stimulated with SiO₂ were treated with BMSCs-conditioned medium (BMSCs-CM) at varying concentrations, and ZC3H4 expression was assessed. As shown in Fig.  7 B and D, Western blot analysis and immunofluorescence staining revealed that ZC3H4 expression was significantly upregulated in macrophages upon SiO₂ stimulation compared to controls. Treatment with BMSCs-CM led to a marked reduction in ZC3H4 expression in a concentration-dependent manner (Fig.  7 C).
