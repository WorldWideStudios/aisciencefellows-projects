L1:Enhanced therapeutic efficacy of repeated bone marrow–derived MSC administration in a murine model of pulmonary fibrosis
L11:A murine silicosis model was analyzed by single-cell RNA sequencing, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) cytokine assays, and human Bone Marrow-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (hBMSCs) transcriptomics after BALF exposure. Mice received either single or repeated intratracheal hBMSCs doses. Cell retention, lung function, imaging, histology, and fibrosis markers were assessed. The role of ZC3H4 in macrophage activation was examined by in vivo expression profiling, in vitro knockdown, and functional assays.
L23:In our previous study, we utilized a novel gold nanoparticle suitable for CT/NIRF dual-modal imaging to label and track bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs). These labeled BMSCs could be tracked for up to 21 days post-transplantation in vivo using NIRF and CT imaging. The transplanted BMSCs were observed to downregulate silica-induced macrophage activation, exhibiting anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic effects in a silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis mouse model [ 3 ]. Nevertheless, these therapeutic benefits are often transient, as MSCs exhibit inherent immunogenicity and limited engraftment capacity, leading to rapid clearance by host immune mechanisms regardless of the delivery route, either intravenous or local. [ 4 ]. To overcome this limitation, accumulating preclinical evidence indicates that while a single administration of MSCs can mitigate fibrosis, repeated dosing may be required to sustain and amplify therapeutic efficacy. For instance, human embryonic stem cell-derived immunity- and matrix-regulatory cells (IMRCs) exhibited marked anti-fibrotic effects following a single infusion, but these effects declined significantly by day 6 [ 5 ]. A dual-dosing regimen—administered on day 1 and day 7—resulted in improved cell persistence and greater histological and functional recovery [ 6 ]. Similarly, repeated administration of MSCs derived from adipose tissue or menstrual blood has been shown to produce superior outcomes in experimental models of pulmonary fibrosis compared to single-dose treatment [ 7 – 9 ]. Notably, studies in cardiac injury models have reported analogous findings: repeated stem cell delivery led to enhanced functional recovery and reduced fibrosis, even when the total cell dose was the same as in single-dose groups. These observations reinforce the concept that treatment efficacy is influenced not only by cell type and delivery route, but also by dosing strategy. Together, these findings support a repeated dosing approach as a means to achieve durable therapeutic benefit in stem cell-based interventions for pulmonary fibrosis.
L36:Human BMSCs isolated from bone marrow aspirates were purchased from Nanjing Drum Tower Clinical Stem Cell Center. The cells were cultured in T75 flasks in DMEM/F12 containing 10% FBS, penicillin (100 U/mL), streptomycin (100 μg/mL), and 2 mM l-glutamine at 37 °C in a 5% CO 2  atmosphere. The medium was changed every three days. BMSCs at P3-8 were used for all experiments.
L40:Male C57BL/6 mice(6–8 weeks old, ~ 30 g) were randomly selected from cages and allocated into four groups (n = 6 per group): control, SiO₂, SiO₂ + SD BMSCs, and SiO₂ + RD BMSCs. After anesthesia induced by pentobarbital sodium (1%, 50 mg/kg), mice were intratracheally administered 100 μL of a silica suspension, except for those in the control group, which received 100 μL of saline. Mice in the control and SiO₂ groups were intratracheally administered 100 μL of sterile saline. In the SiO₂ + SD BMSCs group, 1 × 10⁶ bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) suspended in 100 μL of saline were administered via intratracheal instillation on day 7 post-silica exposure. In the SiO₂ + RD BMSCs group, mice received intratracheal instillations of BMSCs (1 × 10⁶ cells in 100 μL) on both day 7 and day 28 following silica administration. On day 56 after model induction, all animals were humanely euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of an overdose of sodium pentobarbital (≥ 150 mg/kg) to minimize the pain, and lung tissues were subsequently collected for downstream analyses. Animal experiments executed in this study were approved by the Animal Experimental Ethical Inspection Form of Southeast University (approval no. 20220228013). The work has been reported in line with the ARRIVE guidelines 2.0.
L71:To further elucidate how this inflammatory environment affects the function of transplanted stem cells at the molecular level, we conducted transcriptomic profiling of human Bone Marrow-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells (hBMSCs) exposed to early-stage inflammatory conditions. On day 7 post-instillation, BALF was collected from SiO₂-exposed mice and applied to cultured hBMSCs in vitro. Total RNA was extracted from the treated hBMSCs and subjected to absolute quantitative transcriptome sequencing (Fig.  2 B). GO enrichment analysis of the sequencing data revealed significant upregulation of pathways associated with cell division, DNA repair, and cell cycle progression. KEGG pathway analysis further confirmed the enrichment of genes involved in the cell cycle, motor proteins, and cellular senescence (Fig.  2 C, D). In contrast, pathways related to signal transduction, cell adhesion, and cellular responses to hypoxia were significantly downregulated (Fig.  2 E, F). These transcriptomic alterations suggest that exposure to the early inflammatory microenvironment may drive abnormal proliferation of hBMSCs and compromise their stemness, ultimately impairing their reparative function. Additionally, the downregulation of response-related pathways may limit their capacity to survive, adapt, and engraft effectively in vivo, thereby reducing therapeutic efficacy. Together, these findings highlight the limitations of single-dose administration during the early inflammatory phase and provide a rationale for implementing a repeated dosing strategy to enhance stem cell persistence and therapeutic efficacy. Therefore, we designed repeated administration strategy to overcome the limitations of early-phase transplantation, aiming to enhance stem cell persistence and improve therapeutic outcomes (Fig.  3 A). L102:Recent studies have identified ZC3H4, a novel CCCH-type zinc finger protein, as a pivotal regulator in the pathogenesis of silica-induced pulmonary fibrosis. In alveolar epithelial cells, ZC3H4 facilitates epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) via endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, with its expression modulated through the circZC3H4/miR-212 axis [ 25 ]. In macrophages, ZC3H4 contributes to proinflammatory activation and, through paracrine interactions, enhances fibroblast proliferation and migration [ 26 ]. Infiltrating monocytes exhibit reduced anti-fibrotic activity under the influence of ZC3H4, primarily due to suppressed IL-10 secretion via autophagy [ 27 ]. Meanwhile, in pulmonary fibroblasts, ZC3H4 activates a Sigmar1-dependent ER stress pathway that promotes myofibroblast activation and forms a self-reinforcing feedback loop [ 28 ]. Notably, ZC3H4 also plays a role in silica-induced endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT), again mediated by ER stress and autophagy [ 29 ]. In line with these findings, our study demonstrates that bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) can attenuate macrophage activation by downregulating ZC3H4, thereby indirectly limiting fibroblast migration. Collectively, these results underscore the central role of ZC3H4 in coordinating fibrotic signaling across multiple cellular compartments and highlight its potential as a therapeutic target in silicosis.
