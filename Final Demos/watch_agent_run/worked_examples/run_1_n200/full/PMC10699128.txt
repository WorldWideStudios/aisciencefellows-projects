L1:Depletion of Bone Marrow Hematopoietic Cells in  Ebolavirus -Infected Rhesus Macaques
L7:The pathophysiology of long-recognized hematologic abnormalities in  Ebolavirus  (EBOV) disease (EVD) is unknown. From limited human sampling (of peripheral blood), it has been postulated that emergency hematopoiesis plays a role in severe EVD, but the systematic characterization of the bone marrow (BM) has not occurred in human disease or in nonhuman primate models. In a lethal rhesus macaque model of EVD, 18 sternal BM samples exposed to the Kikwit strain of EBOV were compared to those from uninfected controls ( n  = 3). Immunohistochemistry, RNAscope  in situ  hybridization, transmission electron microscopy, and confocal microscopy showed that EBOV infects BM monocytes/macrophages and megakaryocytes. EBOV exposure was associated with severe BM hypocellularity, including depletion of myeloid, erythroid, and megakaryocyte hematopoietic cells. These depletions were negatively correlated with cell proliferation (Ki67 expression) and were not associated with BM apoptosis during disease progression. In EBOV-infected rhesus macaques with terminal disease, BM showed marked hemophagocytosis, megakaryocyte emperipolesis, and the release of immature hematopoietic cells into the sinusoids. Collectively, these data demonstrate not only direct EBOV infection of BM monocytes/macrophages and megakaryocytes but also that disease progression is associated with hematopoietic failure, notably in peripheral cytopenia. These findings inform current pathophysiologic unknowns and suggest a crucial role for BM dysfunction and/or failure, including emergency hematopoiesis, as part of the natural history of severe human disease.

At 4 dpe onward, numerous BM cells were positive for EBOV-VP40 ( Figure 1 C), -GP antigen, and genomic RNA ( Figure 1 D) on IHC and  in situ  hybridization, respectively. L38:As the disease progressed, the most significant histopathologic observation was generalized hypocellularity of the BM, which became moderate to severe at 5 dpe (D5) and thereafter ( Figure 3 , A–C). Compared to controls, BM cords were more conspicuous, with dilated sinusoids lined by prominent, elongated endothelial cells and decreased cellularity of hematopoietic elements. In addition, BM cords presented a loosely arranged stroma with edema, fibrin, microhemorrhage, and many monocytes/macrophages compared to D0 controls. As BM hypocellularity advanced, a majority of the remaining hematopoietic cells were immature myeloid precursors characterized by hypolobulated nuclei (pseudo-Pelger form), irregular cell borders, and an increased nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio ( Figure 3 , D and E). Consistently, on semi-quantification by image analysis (SQIA), BM cellularity dropped at 3 dpe (D3) onward to 5 dpe (D5;  P  < 0.05), 6 dpe (D6;  P  < 0.01), and T ( P  < 0.01) compared to D0 controls ( Figure 3 F). L41:To investigate the impact of EBOV infection on BM progenitor cells, CD71, MPO, and CD41 +  cells were quantified by histopathology and IHC analysis. BM CD71 +  erythroid cells decreased markedly at 3 dpe and then dropped to undetectable levels in T RhMs ( Figure 4 , A–C). The remaining erythroid cells displayed features of less dense, eccentric nuclei, and more eosinophilic cytoplasm. SQIA confirmed that the mean number of CD71 +  cells decreased significantly at D5, D6, and T compared to D0 controls ( P  < 0.001,  P  < 0.001, and  P  < 0.0001, respectively) ( Figure 4 D). Anemia was detected in these EBOV-infected RhMs, 19  although there was no statistically significant correlation between the peripheral red blood cell count and BM CD71 +  cells (data not shown). However, EBOV-infected RhMs presented with significant reticulocytopenia at D4, D5, D6, and T ( P  = 0.0072,  P  < 0.0001,  P  < 0.0003, and  P  < 0.0001) ( Figure 4 E). BM CD71 +  cell count and the peripheral reticulocyte count were positively correlated ( P  < 0.0001,  r  = 0.935) ( Figure 4 F). L48:A number of histopathologic findings in the BM of EBOV-infected RhMs, especially at T, were particularly noteworthy. First, MK emperipolesis increased markedly at 5 dpe and onward, although they were observed occasionally in the BMs of D0 monkeys. In comparison to D0 controls, in which MKs usually engulfed a single cell, the MKs from T RhMs engulfed more cells, ranging from three to eight immature myeloid and/or erythroid cells ( Figure 7 A), and were EBOV-VP35 antigen positive ( Figure 2 C). SQIA confirmed that the percentages of MKs with emperipolesis were significantly greater at D5, D6, and T compared to D0 controls (all,  P  < 0.0001) ( Figure 7 B). Second, many immature MKs and myeloid cells (immature hemopoietic cells) were observed within dilated BM sinusoids, especially in BM from monkeys with end-stage EVD. These MKs presented with hypolobulated, round, and compact nuclei occupying almost the entire cytoplasm ( Figure 7 C), and were also EBOV-VP40 antigen positive ( Figure 2 A). Another remarkable histopathologic finding, as observed in a previous study, 19  was a marked increase in hemophagocytosis, where numerous activated macrophages phagocytized one or more myeloid and/or erythroid precursor cells in their swollen cytoplasm ( Figure 7 D).
