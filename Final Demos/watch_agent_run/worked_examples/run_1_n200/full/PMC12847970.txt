L1:Sirtuin 3 promotes osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells by regulating macrophage polarization under high glucose
L10:Under high glucose conditions, the bone repair process is impaired, in which macrophage polarization plays a critical role. This study aims to investigate the role of the Sirtuin 3/Forkhead box O3a (SIRT3/FoxO3a) pathway in regulating macrophage polarization under hyperglycemic conditions and its impact on the osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs). By establishing an in vitro high-glucose model in RAW264.7 macrophages, we found that high glucose suppressed the SIRT3/FoxO3a pathway, leading to increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, reduced autophagy activity, and promoted polarization of macrophages toward the M1 phenotype. In contrast, SIRT3 overexpression reversed these effects, promoting macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype. When BMSCs were co-cultured with conditioned medium from macrophages, the medium derived from SIRT3-overexpressing macrophages significantly enhanced the proliferation, migration, and osteogenic differentiation capacity of BMSCs. The findings suggest that targeting the SIRT3/FoxO3a pathway may represent a promising novel strategy for improving bone healing in diabetic patients.
L15:Current studies emphasize the pivotal role of the immune microenvironment in osteogenesis 5 . As primary effector cells in the inflammatory cascade, macrophages emerge as a central component of innate immunity that orchestrates bone regeneration in concert with stem cells 6 . Macrophages exhibit dual roles during bone healing. In the early stages, macrophages are activated to the M1 phenotype, which participates in the phagocytosis of pathogens and cell fragments. While in the later stages of fracture healing, macrophages switch from M1 to M2 phenotype, promoting anti-inflammation and angiogenesis, and enhancing osteogenesis mediated by bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) 7 . Notably, DM is a chronic inflammatory disorder that triggers excessive M1 activation in macrophages, impairing the transition from pro-inflammatory to anti-inflammatory states and compromising bone healing 8 , 9 . Therefore, strategies to modulate macrophage polarization represent a promising therapeutic avenue for improving diabetic bone integration.

L39:To investigate the effects of MCM on the proliferation and migration of BMSCs, scratch assays and CCK-8 assays were performed. BMSCs cultured in OE-SIRT3 MCM exhibited slightly increased migration on day 1 and significantly enhanced migration on day 3 compared to those in Vector MCM (Fig.  6 A-B). CCK-8 results showed superior proliferation of BMSCs in the OE-SIRT3 MCM, with significantly higher cell viability on days 3 and 5 (Fig.  6 C), indicating that OE-SIRT3 macrophages create a pro-regenerative microenvironment via paracrine signaling. L62:BMSCs were isolated from 1–2-week-old male C57BL/6 J mice (4–6 g; Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd, China) using a bone fragment digestion method 43 . Briefly, mice were deeply anesthetized via inhalation of 5% isoflurane, and after confirmation of absence of toe pinch reflex, euthanasia was performed by cervical dislocation. The bodies were then sterilized in 75% ethanol for 3–5 min. Femurs and tibiae were aseptically dissected, and adherent soft tissues were removed by gentle rubbing on sterile gauze. After excising the metaphysis, the bone marrow cavity was repeatedly flushed with DMEM until it turned white. Bones were meticulously cut into 1–3 mm 3  fragments and transferred to a 50 ml centrifuge tube containing 1 mg/ml collagenase II (Gibco, USA) diluted in MSC PRO medium (Xirui Infinite, China). The fragments were digested in a shaking incubator at 37 °C and 200 rpm for 1 h. The bone fragments were plated in a 25 cm 2  culture dish and cultured in MSC PRO medium, which was changed every 3 days. Cells were passaged after reaching 70–80% confluence, and the third generation of BMSCs was used for subsequent experiments. This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of The Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University (No. 20220661), with all procedures complying strictly with the ARRIVE guidelines and institutional animal care regulations.
