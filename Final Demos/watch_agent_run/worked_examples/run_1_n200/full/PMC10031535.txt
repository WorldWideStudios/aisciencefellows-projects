L29:Human Monocyte-Derived Macrophages (hMDMs) are obtained with a density gradient centrifugation using a Ficoll-gradient method. This method relies on the different densities of blood components for their separation in distinct phases. The Ficoll solution allows the partition of erythrocytes and polynuclear cells at the lower part of the tube due to their density being higher than Ficoll while plasma and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) lay on top of it. Monocytes (CD14+ cells) can then be isolated from PBMCs by positive magnetic sorting. Subsequently they can be differentiated into human primary macrophages, hMDMs, by culturing them in the presence of recombinant human M-CSF (rh-MCSF) ( Figure 1 ). 1. Cell isolation: Separation of PBMCs from whole blood (or buffy coat). a. Dilute blood with equal volume of PBS (at room temperature, RT). b. Add 15 mL of Lymphocytes Separation Medium in 50 mL Falcon tubes (RT). c. Pipette slowly 30 mL of PBS-diluted blood over the separation medium, be careful not to mix both phases. Note:  For volumes less than 30 mL of PBS-diluted blood, use a Ficoll to blood ratio of 1:2 and adjust all volumes accordingly. d. Centrifuge tubes for 30 min at RT and 300 ×  g , with an acceleration of 2 and without breaks. e. Retrieve plasma and the PBMC layers (the white band above the Ficoll band, see  Figure 1 ), transfer to new separate 50 mL tubes and add PBS to a final volume of 45 mL. f. Centrifuge cells during 10 min at 300 ×  g  and 10°C. g. Discard supernatant, pool all pellets, add PBS to a final volume of 45 mL and centrifuge cells during 10 min at 300 ×  g  and 10°C. h. Resuspend the pellet in 2 mL of cold MACS buffer and count the cells with Trypan Blue. i. Transfer the cells to a 15 mL falcon tube, spin down the cells and resuspend in 8 μL of MACS buffer for each  1 ∗ 10 6  cells. j. Incubate the cells on ice for 5 min to prevent the internalization of CD14 magnetic beads by the phagocytic cells. 2. Cell isolation: Separation of Monocytes from PBMCS by positive magnetic sorting. a. Add CD14 magnetic beads to the cells with a volume of 2 μL for each  1 ∗ 10 6  cells. b. Incubate at 4°C for 15 min with gentle agitation on a roller shaker set at 40 rpm. c. Add 5 mL of MACS buffer right after incubation to dilute the beads and avoid non-specific binding. d. Centrifuge cells during 10 min at 300 ×  g  at 10°C. In the meantime, prepare the column and magnet by placing the column on the magnet and the filter on top of the column. e. Wash column with 3 mL of MACS buffer. f. Remove supernatant after centrifuging and resuspend the pellet in 5 μL of MACS buffer for each  1 ∗ 10 6  cells then directly transfer the cells on the column. g. Wait until the last drop and then proceed with 3 washes of the column with 3 mL of MACS buffer. h. Remove column from the magnet and, at a recommended distance of 30 cm from the magnetic field, add 5 mL of MACS buffer to the column and flush the contents with a plunger into a 15 mL tube. i. Add cells to a new column without filter and repeat steps e, g, and h. CRITICAL:  Using two columns improves the purity of the cells and avoids the presence of red blood cells. j. Count cells. k. Centrifuge cells during 10 min at 300 ×  g  at 10°C and discard supernatant. 3. Cell culture: Differentiation of Monocytes into Macrophages. a. Prepare medium for differentiation; X-VIVO-15 or RPMI10%FBS supplemented with 25 ng/mL of rh-MCSF and warm it up to 37°C. b. Plate cells either in 10 cm Nunc™ UpCell™ dishes (maximum of  8 ∗ 10 6  in 12 mL) or in 6-well Nunc™ UpCell™ plates (maximum of  1 ∗ 10 6  cells in 2 mL per well) depending on the obtained cell count. CRITICAL:  The detachment technique is important to maintain the cell-integrity of the macrophages. Using thermosensible dishes such as Nunc™ UpCell™ plates greatly enhances cell viability and integrity after detachment. c. Keep cells in a humidified incubator at 37°C + 5% CO 2 , d. Three days after plating the cells, add 6 mL of pre-warmed rh-MCSF supplemented culture medium to a 10 cm dish or 1 mL to a well of a 6-well plate. e. Wait until day 6–7 after plating to obtain fully differentiated macrophages. f. Place plates at 20°C for 20 min inside an incubator or at RT on a bench-top for the cells to detach. Cells can be left at RT for up to 40 min if required for improved detachment. g. Collect half of the supernatant and place it in a 15 mL Falcon tube. h. Flush the dish or the well with the remaining medium to further detach the cells and collect them in the falcon tube. i. Add 5 mL and 1 mL of PBS to the dish and well, respectively and repeat the flushing step. j. Collect the supernatant, check with the microscope for good detachment of the cells. Repeat step h if necessary. k. Spin down harvested cells at 400 ×  g  for 10 min at RT, resuspend in preferred culture medium without phenol red and count cells. Pause point:  One can continue with the experiment or stop at this point and freeze the hMDMs in 1 mL of CryoStor® CS10 freezing medium or in 1 mL of 10% DMSO in FCS, at a cell concentration between  0.5  and  10 ∗ 10 6  cells/mL. Differentiated hMDMs can be conserved in liquid nitrogen for several months. Figure 1 Generation of human monocyte-derived macrophages (hMDMs)
