L18:Auricular chondrocyte-derived extracellular vesicles promoted the production of interleukin-10 in bone marrow M1-like macrophages but reduced CD206 expression.
L23:Macrophages are a type of immune cell that is differentiated from monocytes. They play roles in innate immunity by recognizing and phagocytosing foreign pathogens. Additionally, they function as antigen-presenting cells, whereby they degrade phagocytosed foreign substances with digestive enzymes and present antigenic information to T cells, thereby promoting an acquired immune response. Macrophages are classified into two main categories: classically activated M1 and alternatively activated M2. M1 macrophages secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1 beta and TNF-alpha, whereas M2 macrophages have anti-inflammatory and tissue repair functions [ 6 ]. M2 can be further classified into M2a, M2b, M2c, and M2d, all of which commonly express interleukin-10 (IL-10) [ 7 , 8 ]. In contrast, macrophages that cannot be classified by conventional methods due to their possession of both M1 and M2 characteristics have been documented in the literature [ 9 ]. Macrophages derived from monocytes and stimulated by granulocyte-macrophage colony stimulating factor (GM-CSF) are referred to as M1-like macrophages. These macrophages have been demonstrated to alleviate inflammation and promote chondrogenesis [ [10] ,  [11] ,  [12] ,  [13] ]. However, the mechanisms underlying the anti-inflammatory and cartilage formation-promoting effects remain largely unexplored, while numerous studies examining the role of macrophages in cartilage degradation and its contribution to arthritis [ [14] ,  [15] ,  [16] ,  [17] ,  [18] ].
L25:Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are granule-like structures that are covered by a phospholipid bilayer. EVs have been observed to contain microRNAs and a variety of proteins [ 26 , 27 ]. It is thought that they play a role in intercellular communication. EVs are produced by a variety of cells and have been identified in a range of bodily fluids, including serum, urine, and saliva. EVs with a diameter of less than 200 nm are referred to as small EVs (sEVs). Those derived from endosomes are designated as exosomes [ 28 ]. Recently, reports have emerged concerning the anti-inflammatory, chondroprotective, and cartilage repair actions of EVs [ [29] ,  [30] ,  [31] ,  [32] ,  [33] ]. Nevertheless, the function of extracellular vesicles in the alteration of macrophage polarity and cartilage maturation remains unclear. Previously, our research group has demonstrated that co-culturing chondrocytes with macrophages facilitates chondrogenesis by predominating M2 macrophages [ 12 , 34 ]. It has been demonstrated that soluble factors derived from chondrocytes and macrophages facilitate chondrogenesis [ 35 ]. Accordingly, our research was directed towards elucidating the chondrogenic effects of M1-like macrophages and the potential involvement of extracellular vesicles, such as exosomes, in these cell-cell interactions. The present study aims to elucidate a mechanism by which chondrocyte-derived extracellular vesicles influence changes in macrophage characteristics.
L35:Femoral and tibial bones were resected from euthanized 6-week-old male C57BL/6J mice and bone marrow was collected by flushing methods reported before [ 12 ]. Briefly, bone marrow was flushed with DPBS- by using syringes and needles. A 70 μm pore cell strainer filtrated outpouring cells. Cells precipitated by centrifugation at 500G for 3 min were resuspended in RBC lysis buffer (144 mmol/l NH 4 Cl, 17 mmol/l Tris buffer) to be hemolyzed. After that, cells were cultivated in DMEM high-glucose medium supplemented with 20 ng/ml macrophage-colony stimulating factor (M-CSF), 10 % fetal bovine serum, and 1 % penicillin and streptomycin at 37 °C and 5 % CO 2 .
L36:Twenty-four hours later, floating cells were collected and seeded. To be differentiated into M1-like macrophages, cells were incubated in DMEM high-glucose medium supplemented with 20 ng/ml recombinant mouse granulocyte-macrophage colony stimulating factor (rmGM-CSF) (FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation, Japan), 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin and streptomycin for 7 days at 37 °C and 5 % CO 2 .
L37:The induction of other subtypes is as follows: macrophages induced with 20 ng/ml rmGM-CSF for one week and 100 ng/ml lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 24 h at the end of induction were designated M1, those induced with 50 ng/ml M-CSF were designated M2-like and those induced with 50 ng/ml M-CSF for one week and 20 ng/ml IL-4 for 24 h at the end of induction were designated M2.
L39:Co-cultures of auricular chondrocytes and bone marrow-derived macrophages were performed in DMEM high-glucose medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin and streptomycin. Macrophages were seeded at a density of 5.0 e+5 cells/well in a 6-well plate, and 1.0 e+5 cells of auricular chondrocytes were seeded on the membrane of a cell culture insert (ThinCert®, Greiner Bio-One) with a pore size of 0.4 μm. Neutral sphingomyelinase 2 (nSMase2) inhibitor was used to pharmacologically inhibit sEVs in chondrocytes. Before co-culture with macrophages, chondrocytes were pre-incubated with 20 μM GW4869 (Sigma-Aldrich) at 37 °C for 2 h. Only dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added to the pre-incubation of control chondrocytes in the same volume as the GW4869 solution. The medium was changed twice a week for 1 week. After one week of co-culture, the macrophages were harvested.
L46:Bone marrow monocyte-derived macrophages were seeded in 35 mm non-coated glass bottom dishes (MATSUNAMI GLASS IND., LTD., Osaka, Japan) at a density of 8.0 e+5 cells/dish. Cells were stained with FITC anti-mouse F4/80 Antibody, (Cat:123108, BioLegend) or phalloidin and fixed in a 4 % paraformaldehyde phosphate buffer solution. After membrane permeabilization with 0.5 % Triton X-100, cell nuclei were stained with Hoechst 33342 (DOJINDO LABORATORIES, Kumamoto, Japan) and the IL-10 Monoclonal Antibody, APC (Cat:17-7101- 81, eBioscience™ Inc. Inc.) was used to stain for intracellular IL-10.

L76:The findings of this study indicate that ACEVs can stimulate the production of IL-10 in bone marrow monocytes-derived M1-like macrophages. A detailed analysis of the uptake of ACEVs into macrophages and the resulting changes in macrophage phenotype revealed insights into the interaction between ACEVs and macrophages.
L79:Regarding the effect of EVs on cytokine secretion in macrophages, it has been demonstrated that EVs derived from articular chondrocytes enhance IL-10 production by RAW264.7, a macrophage-like cell line [ 31 ]. As a preliminary experiment, we added ACEVs to the RAW264.7 cell culture medium and observed changes in the gene expression of IL-10. However, no significant increase in IL-10 expression was observed upon the addition of ACEVs (data not shown). The disparate expression in RAW264.7 cells from M1-like macrophages may be attributed to the distinction in the provenance of chondrocytes from joints or auricles and the heterogeneity of the cell population present in the bone marrow, namely the interactions between cells in the fraction other than monocytes and macrophages.
L87:In conclusion, this study demonstrated that ACEVs modulate inflammatory cytokine production by macrophages differentiated from bone marrow monocytes. Fluid-mediated crosstalk between chondrocytes and macrophages results in the polarization of macrophages towards an anti-inflammatory phenotype. Subsequently, it was proposed that macrophages may facilitate chondrogenesis and contribute to cartilage repair and regeneration by regulating perichondral inflammation. However, this study did not fully clarify the chondrogenic effect of macrophage-derived IL-10. This finding may contribute to improving the quality of regenerated cartilage and the reliability of therapeutic effects by allowing the regenerated cartilage to mature before transplantation in cartilage regenerative medicine.
