L11:While significant advances have been made in understanding renal pathophysiology, less is known about the role of glycosphingolipid (GSL) metabolism in driving organ dysfunction. Here, we used a small molecule inhibitor of glucosylceramide synthase to modulate GSL levels in three mouse models of distinct renal pathologies: Alport syndrome ( Col4a3  KO), polycystic kidney disease ( Nek8 jck ), and steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome ( Nphs2  cKO). At the tissue level, we identified a core immune-enriched transcriptional signature that was shared across models and enriched in human polycystic kidney disease. Single nuclei analysis identified robust transcriptional changes across multiple kidney cell types, including epithelial and immune lineages. To further explore the role of GSL modulation in macrophage biology, we performed in vitro studies with homeostatic and inflammatory bone marrow-derived macrophages. Cumulatively, this study provides a comprehensive overview of renal dysfunction and the effect of GSL modulation on kidney-derived cells in the setting of renal dysfunction.
L47:To interrogate how the effects of GSL lowering may be influenced by macrophage cell state, we performed in vitro experiments with “resting” (endotoxin-naive) and “inflammatory” (LPS-induced) bone marrow-derived (BM) macrophages. First, we verified that GSL levels were measurable in vitro and could be modulated by inhibiting GCS. Indeed, treatment with either GCSi or an alternative small molecule inhibitor (GCSi 2) reduced GL-1 and downstream gangliosides in resting macrophages (Fig.  S10b, c ). Compared to the resting state, we observed an increase of GM-3 in inflammatory-type macrophages, an effect suppressed by GCSi. These data demonstrate that GSL levels in BM macrophages varied by cell state. As an endogenous expression of  Col4a3  and  Nphs2  was absent in BM macrophages, subsequent experiments were conducted with WT or  Nek8 jck  BM macrophages only (Fig.  S11g ). Metabolic reprogramming, defined as shifts in the utilization of oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) versus glycolysis, has been well described in cell state changes during inflammation 27 , 28 . To interrogate how GSL lowering impacts energy production, we measured oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) in both resting and inflammatory-type macrophages. As expected, the OCR/ECAR ratio was significantly lower in inflammatory-type cells (relative to resting), indicating increased reliance on glycolysis for ATP production (Fig.  5a ). GCSi did not have a robust effect on resting cells, but further lowered OCR/ECAR under inflammatory conditions. The addition of betacellulin (BTC), an ERBB4 ligand, did not have any further effect on this phenotype, suggesting that it may be independent of HER signaling (Fig.  5a ). Previously, IL-6 cytokine release was reported to be modulated by GCS inhibition 27 . In BM macrophages, GCSi significantly increased IL-6 secretion in WT and  Nek8 jck  inflammatory macrophages (Fig.  5b ). This response was not influenced by BTC stimulation. To validate the specificity of the GCSi effect on IL-6 induction in macrophages, we repeated the experiment with an alternative GCS inhibitor (GCSi 2) and observed similar results (Fig.  S10a ). Moreover,  Tnf  expression was increased by  Ugcg  knockdown in BM macrophages, validating the changes observed in the transcriptomic analysis (Fig.  S12 ). L125:Differentiated macrophages were induced with 10 ng/ml lipopolysaccharide at 37 °C for 3 hours, subsequently, the macrophages were treated with vehicle (DMSO) or 300 nM GCS inhibitors (Eliglustat (Sigma, SML3212), Genz-667161 or venglustat (aka GZ402671)) for 48 hours in complete media supplemented with 25 ng/ml m-CSF and 10 ng/ml LPS at 37 °C. Following treatment, RNA was extracted from the cells using RNeasy Plus Micro Kit (Qiagen, 74034). RNA quality was determined using Agilent RNA 6000 Pico Kit (Agilent, 5067-1513) with the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer according to manufacturer’s instructions. Bulk RNA sequencing libraries were prepared using Illumina Stranded mRNA Prep according to manufacturer’s instructions. Library QC and concentration were performed and determined using High Sensitivity D5000 ScreenTape Assay (Agilent). Following manufacturer’s instructions. Libraries were subsequently pooled and sequenced on the NovaSeq 6000 platform with the following parameters: Read 1: 149 bp, Read 2: 149 bp, Indices: 10 bp. Data analysis was completed using Omicsoft Array Studio. On average, each sample was sequenced at a depth of 55 million paired-end reads. Illumina adaptors were trimmed during the CL to FastQ conversion. All raw data was QC’d using the “Raw Data QC” function within Array Studio. Poor-quality reads were filtered out using a Q score cutoff of 20. Following the above filter criteria, about 49 million paired-end reads per sample were uniquely mapped to the B39 Mouse Reference Genome using the Ensembl.R109 Gene Model. Raw counts were then converted to FPKM using the “Report Gene/Transcript Counts” function in Array Studio. This data was used in all downstream analyses. Differential expression analysis was performed using DESeq2 in R (DESeq2 version: 1.40.0, R version 4.3.0) 62 . Comparison pathway analysis was completed using log 2 fold change and adjusted p-values obtained from differential expression analysis and imported into Ingenuity (Qiagen). Distinct pathological pathways and cell type analyses were conducted using differentially expressed genes and imported into Enrichr for large gene lists or MSigDB 23 – 25 , 49 – 51 .
