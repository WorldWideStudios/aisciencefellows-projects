L12:Macrophages play differential roles in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis due to their different phenotypes. Although α-SMA +  macrophages have been found to present in bone marrow and atherosclerotic plaques, their role in atherosclerosis remains unclear. By performing partial carotid ligation (PCL) on monocyte/macrophage lineage-tracked mice, we observed bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages in the subendothelium and atherosclerotic plaques under disturbed flow conditions. The functional role of α-SMA +  macrophages in atherosclerotic plaque formation was examined using macrophage-specific  Acta2  knockout ( Acta2 MKO ) mice generated by crossing  Acta2 f/f  transgenic mice with  LysM-Cre  mice. The size of the aortic plaques was 77.43% smaller in  Acta2 MKO  mice than in  Acta2 f/f  mice following adeno-associated virus-mutant  PCSK9  injection and high-fat diet (HFD) feeding for 12 weeks. A significant reduction in lipid deposition, macrophage infiltration and the α-SMA +  area was observed in the aortic roots of  Acta2 MKO  mice compared with  Acta2 f/f  mice. L17:Macrophages exhibit different roles in atherosclerosis and are largely influenced by their polarization into different phenotypes. Typically, macrophages polarize into proinflammatory M1 or anti-inflammatory M2 phenotypes [ 2 ], which are closely related to their involvement in atherosclerosis development [ 3 ]. In addition to these polarized states, macrophages can also undergo cell transdifferentiation within atherosclerotic plaques. This phenomenon reflects the high plasticity of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) and macrophages in lesions, allowing them to express markers typically associated with other cell types [ 4 ]. Notably, α-smooth muscle actin-positive (α-SMA + ) macrophages have been identified in plaques from high-fat diet-induced atherosclerosis [ 4 ], and similar α-SMA +  macrophages have been observed adjacent to primitive hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) in the bone marrow [ 5 ]. However, it remains unclear whether these macrophages infiltrate the endothelium and contribute to plaque formation, as their role in atherosclerotic plaque formation has yet to be fully characterized.
L18:Atherosclerosis preferentially develops in regions of disturbed blood flow, such as the aortic arch (AA) and arterial branches, where vascular inflammation is prominent. Partial carotid artery ligation (PCL), often combined with high-fat diet (HFD) feeding, is commonly used to study the mechanisms underlying atherosclerosis [ 6 ]. In this study, we performed PCL on monocyte/macrophage lineage tracing mice and identified bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages in the disturbed flow-induced inflamed vascular wall. These macrophages were also observed in both PCL/HFD-induced carotid plaques and traditional HFD-induced aortic plaques. Functionally, macrophage-specific α-SMA deletion led to a significant reduction in plaque size in the aortas of adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mutant  PCSK9 -induced atherosclerotic mice. Further analysis revealed that dysregulated cholesterol uptake and efflux via the AKT pathway in bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages contributed to the progression of atherosclerotic plaque formation.

L28:CX 3 CR1 GFP/ +  and C57BL/6J mice (recipient mice, 8 weeks old) were lethally irradiated at a dose of 7.5 Gy. Bone marrow cells were harvested from the tibiae and femurs of the donor GFP +  mice and injected into the tail vein of the irradiated recipient mice (5 × 10 6  cells per mouse). Peripheral blood was collected for chimerism analysis by flow cytometry.

In these mice, one allele of the  CX 3 CR1  gene, which encodes the receptor for the membrane-tethered chemokine fractalkine (CX3CL1 or Fkn), is replaced with the gene encoding green fluorescent protein (GFP). This results in GFP expression exclusively in circulating CD11b + Gr-1  m  monocytes, but not in neutrophils (Figure  S1 ).

L60:In vascular inflammation, macrophages can be derived from both bone marrow and resident populations, the latter of which are involved in maintaining arterial homeostasis [ 10 ]. Our analysis indicated that bone marrow-derived macrophages in the carotid arteries expressed α-SMA, comprising 11.157% of the total vascular wall cells (Fig.  1 J). To exclude the possibility that resident macrophages contribute to α-SMA +  macrophages infiltrated into the subendothelium, we performed flow cytometry using  CX 3 CR1 GFP/ +  mice with a WT hematopoietic system, which retains GFP in the resident macrophages. This analysis revealed very few GFP + α-SMA +  cells observed in the PCL group at 7 days (Fig.  1 K), confirming that the majority of the α-SMA +  macrophages originated from bone marrow-derived macrophages.

L68:Although we confirmed the presence of α-SMA +  MOMA-2 +  cells in atherosclerotic plaques, which are primarily derived from bone marrow monocytes, the role of macrophage α-SMA in plaque formation has not been explored. To address this, we next investigated whether macrophage-derived α-SMA influences plaque formation using myeloid-specific  Acta2  knockout mice.  Acta2 f/f  transgenic mice were crossed with  LysM-Cre  mice to generate myeloid-specific  Acta2  knockout ( Acta2 MKO ) mice (Fig.  3 A–C). Notably, macrophage-specific  Acta2  knockout did not affect the proportion of monocytes in the peripheral blood (Figure S2A). Consistent with the timeline commonly used in literature [ 11 – 14 ], atherosclerosis was induced via the injection of an adeno-associated virus (AAV8) encoding the Asp374-to-Tyr mutant version of human  PCSK9  ( PCSK9 DY ), followed by 12 weeks of HFD feeding (Fig.  3 D). PCSK9 mutations enhance binding to LDLR, which is widely recognized as a classical animal model of atherosclerosis [ 13 ]. As expected, LDLR levels were almost undetectable in AAV -PCSK9 DY - injected mice (Figure S2B), confirming the successful establishment of the atherosclerotic model. Moreover, macrophage-specific deletion of  Acta2  did not affect body weight (Figure S2C), hematopoietic cell counts (Figure S2D–F) or serum lipid levels (Figure S2G–J) following a 12-week HFD. L88:This study investigated the role of α-SMA +  macrophages in the development of atherosclerosis. We demonstrated that bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages accumulated in the subendothelium of the d-flow inflamed vascular wall and in atherosclerotic plaques. Additionally, macrophage-specific α-SMA deficiency attenuated atherosclerotic plaque formation. We further showed that macrophage α-SMA promoted the expression of the scavenger receptor SR-A, increased Ox-LDL binding and uptake, and decreased the cholesterol transporter ABCA1 potentially via the AKT signaling pathway (Fig.  6 ). These findings underscore the proatherogenic role of α-SMA +  macrophages in atherogenesis, providing potential targets for atherosclerosis prevention and therapy. L89:Macrophages play a central role in the development of atherosclerotic lesions by mediating lipid uptake, particularly Ox-LDL, and by inducing an inflammatory response [ 2 ]. Our study addresses three critical gaps in the current understanding of the role of α-SMA +  macrophages in atherosclerosis. First, although it is known that macrophages in plaques originate from both bone marrow-derived and resident populations [ 18 ], it remains unclear whether bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages infiltrate the endothelium. Using monocyte/macrophage lineage-tracked mice and two atherosclerosis models, we demonstrated that bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages accumulated in both the subendothelium and atherosclerotic plaques. Second, while α-SMA +  macrophages have been reported in bone marrow [ 5 ] and atherosclerotic plaques [ 4 ], the contribution of resident macrophages to the α-SMA +  population in plaques is unclear. Our data definitively show that α-SMA +  macrophages in atherosclerotic plaques are predominantly derived from the bone marrow rather than from resident macrophages. Third, the functional contribution of α-SMA +  macrophages to plaque formation has not been fully elucidated. Our study revealed that macrophage specific α-SMA deficiency inhibited plaque formation, in part by reducing Ox-LDL binding and uptake. This effect is likely mediated through the AKT signaling pathway, which upregulates the scavenger receptor SR-A and downregulates the cholesterol transporter ABCA1.
L90:Macrophages are highly heterogeneous and exhibit plasticity in response to local microenvironments. These cells can be broadly categorized into M1- and M2-like macrophages [ 3 ,  19 ]. M1-like macrophages predominate in the early stages of atherosclerosis and contribute to inflammation, whereas M2-like macrophages assist in plaque resolution and repair [ 15 ]. We identified a new subset of bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages that do not fit the traditional M1/M2 classification. This subpopulation promotes lipid accumulation, inhibits lipid efflux, and supports atherosclerotic plaque formation. While many α-SMA +  macrophages in plaques are traditionally thought to arise from smooth muscle cell transdifferentiation [ 4 ,  20 ], we found that these cells can also originate from bone marrow. Therefore, we propose that atherosclerotic plaque macrophages can be categorized into three types: α-SMA +  macrophages differentiated from smooth muscle cells, α-SMA +  macrophages derived from bone marrow, and resident α-SMA −  macrophages. Our study thus provides a more comprehensive classification of macrophages in plaques and highlights the distinct role of α-SMA +  macrophages in atherosclerosis.
L94:In conclusion, our study revealed that bone marrow-derived α-SMA +  macrophages accumulate in the subendothelium and atherosclerotic plaques. α-SMA expression in macrophages promotes plaque formation by enhancing the expression of SR-A, increasing Ox-LDL uptake, and inhibiting lipid efflux via the AKT pathway. These findings deepen our understanding of the roles of α-SMA +  macrophages in vascular inflammation and atherosclerotic plaque formation and open new avenues for potential therapeutic interventions targeting α-SMA +  macrophages in atherosclerosis.
