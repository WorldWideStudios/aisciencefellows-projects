L1:Diabetic wound healing via a co-delivery system for bone marrow derived stem cells and euphorbia hirta extract: an in vitro and in vivo study
L7:Diabetes mellitus, a persistent metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels, presents a substantial global health challenge due to its escalating prevalence and associated complications. In the current study, a nanofibrous scaffold was developed for the co-delivery of bone marrow-derived stem cells and Euphorbia hirta extract into the diabetic wound beds. Scaffolds were produced using the electrospinning method, and their physicochemical and biological properties were studied in vitro. The wound healing function of the developed delivery system was investigated in a rat model of diabetic wound healing. The study showed that Euphorbia hirta extract-loaded scaffolds imparted no significant toxicity toward L929 cells and promoted their migration activity. A wound healing study showed that scaffolds loaded with both bone marrow-derived stem cells and euphorbia hirta extract had the highest wound healing activity, as shown by significantly higher wound closure percentage, collagen deposition, and epithelial thickness. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent Assay studies showed that these dressings reduced tissue oxidative stress and alleviated inflammatory responses.
L10:Diabetes mellitus, a persistent metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels, presents a substantial global health challenge due to its escalating prevalence and associated complications [ 1 ,  2 ]. A particularly concerning consequence is the impediment of wound healing, notably in individuals with diabetic wounds. These wounds, particularly diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs), are a significant complication of diabetes mellitus, resulting from a complex interplay of neuropathy, vascular insufficiency, immune dysfunction, and impaired wound healing mechanisms. These wounds are difficult to treat and often lead to severe outcomes, such as lower-limb amputations [ 3 ,  4 ]. Peripheral neuropathy is a critical factor in the development of diabetic wounds. Chronic hyperglycemia induces oxidative stress and glycosylation of nerve proteins, leading to nerve damage. This damage manifests in sensory, motor, and autonomic dysfunctions, reducing pain perception, causing muscle imbalances, and impairing sweat gland function [ 5 ,  6 ]. Diabetes is associated with peripheral arterial disease (PAD), which exacerbates ischemia in the lower extremities. Hyperglycemia promotes endothelial dysfunction, vasoconstriction, and arterial wall thickening due to atherosclerosis. These changes impair blood flow, reducing oxygen and nutrient delivery to wounds [ 4 ,  7 ]. Additionally, hypercoagulability and reduced vasodilatory responses further hinder circulation. L18:The ExtractPCLCOL and PCLCOL scaffolds underwent sterilization using 70% ethanol for 60 minutes, followed by several washes with DMEM-F12 media (Invitrogen, USA). Subsequently, L929 cells (Pasteur Institute, Tehran, Iran) were seeded onto the scaffolds at a density of 10,000 cells/cm 2  and cultured for a duration of 8 days. Alamar blue assays were performed on days 2, 4, and 8 to compare cell viability between those cultured on tissue culture plates and those on the scaffolds. In brief, a mixture of 1 ml Alamar blue solution (Invitrogen, USA) with 9 ml DMEM-F12 media containing 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics was prepared. This solution (350 µl) was applied to the scaffolds and incubated for 4 hours. The absorbance of various samples was subsequently measured at 570 nm using a plate reader (Perkins Elmer, Finland). Each experiment was repeated three times.
L20:L929 cells were seeded on the ExtractPCLCOL and PCLCOL scaffolds at 40,000 cells/cm 2  density in 96-well plates and cultured for 48 hours. Subsequently, a 1% v/v H 2 O 2  solution was introduced into each well, and the cells were then incubated for 60 minutes. Then, the media in each well was discarded, and 350 µL of the Alamar blue solution (Invitrogen, USA) prepared in DMEM-F12 media containing 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics was added onto the scaffolds and incubated for 4 hours. Finally, the absorbance of the samples was read at 570 nm. Each experiment was repeated three times. Control is the cells cultured on tissue culture plate with DMEM-F12 media containing 10% FBS and 1% antibiotics.
L32:The in vitro migration of L929 cells in response to ExtractPCLCOL and PCLCOL scaffold extracts was evaluated through a migration assay. In summary, 200 mg of each scaffold was immersed in 10 ml of DMEM-F12 culture media and incubated at 37 °C on a rotary shaker for 5 days. Following incubation, the scaffolds were removed, and the media were filtered. Subsequently, L929 cells were cultivated in 24-well plates until reaching confluence, and a linear scratch was created at the midline of each well using a sterile pipette. After washing away cellular debris with PBS, the cells were exposed to the scaffold extracts for 48 hours. The wound area was photographed on days 0 and 2, and Image J software was employed for the calculation of in vitro wound closure.

L84:Electrospun scaffolds offer a promising approach for delivering cells and bioactive agents to enhance wound healing. These nanofibrous structures provide a conducive environment, promoting cell attachment and controlled release of therapeutic agents, ultimately facilitating tissue regeneration in the wound bed for more effective and accelerated healing processes [ 18 ,  28 ]. In the current research, we developed a delivery system for Euphorbia hirta extract and BMSCs in order to improve diabetic wound healing in a rat model. Our microstructural images revealed a notable similarity between this system and the ECM found in native tissues. This similarity implies that the system has the capacity to replicate the natural environment, potentially influencing cellular behavior to mimic physiological conditions [ 29 ]. Biocompatibility study revealed that none of the extract-loaded or extract-free scaffolds were toxic against fibroblast cells. Besides, the ExtractPCLCOL constructs protected these cells against oxidative stress. This result could be attributed to the presence of euphorbia hirta extract in the matrix of PCLCOL scaffolds. In this context, research conducted on Wistar rats indicated that the euphorbia hirta extract effectively reinstated antioxidant activities and hepatic redox capacity. This suggests a protective impact against oxidative stress induced by carbon tetrachloride [ 30 ]. This result was further validated by the results of DPPH assay that showed that euphorbia hirta extract significantly augmented the radical scavenging capability of PCLCOL scaffolds
