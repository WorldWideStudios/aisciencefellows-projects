L12:Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most common primary central nervous system tumor with a dismal prognosis and limited treatment options. Recent success utilizing immunotherapies for treating other solid tumors have been largely unsuccessful in GBM. One of the primary mechanisms of GBM immunotherapeutic resistance is because of excessive infiltration of myeloid cells that create an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME). Among these infiltrating myeloid cells, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), comprise a substantial portion of the TME and are associated with poor prognosis in GBM patients. Researchers have only recently begun to dissect the dynamics and complexity of TAMs. However, reliable and reproducible translational methods for generating GBM TAMs  in vitro  are lacking. Here, we have investigated an  in vitro,  reproducible murine-based model for bone marrow-derived, glioma-educated macrophages (gTAMs) and performed rigorous analysis to expand our understanding of gTAMs to provide a validated tool for investigating therapeutic response.

L19:Access to patient-derived TAMs can be difficult for cancer researchers to obtain owing to sample availability, cell viability, tissue preservation, sampling bias, and inherent difficulties in isolating and culturing primary, differentiated myeloid cells  in vitro. 13  Additionally, patient-derived cell lines or lysates can contain stromal cells such as fibroblasts, astrocytes, and immune cells, which complicate experiments by introducing multiple sources for tumor-modifying mediators. Further, the isolation of TAMs from patient and murine GBM tumors for downstream analysis or culturing is complicated because of the difficulty of deciphering infiltrating BMDM from resident microglia. 13  Although previous studies have utilized gTAMs  in vitro  for hypothesis-driven research, the phenotype and functionality of glioma-educated bone marrow-derived macrophages have not been deeply analyzed; hence, the validity and utility of these approaches as a model reflective of human biology are unclear. 23-26
L20:Benner et al. described the differentiation of human peripheral monocytes into TAMs using a combination of cytokines (M-CSF, IL-4, and IL-10) and tumor-conditioned media (TCM) from breast cancer and lymphoma cell lines. 27  The cytokines IL-4 and IL-10, produced by stromal immune cells, including regulatory T cells (Tregs) and existing TAMs, promote BMDM polarization into TAMs, therefore these cytokines were added to the culture media to replicate the GBM TME. 28 , 29  Here, we developed an  in vitro,  murine-based model for bone marrow-derived, glioma-educated macrophages (gTAMs) and performed a rigorous analysis to expand our understanding of the resulting gTAMs to provide a validated tool for investigating therapeutic responses.

L47:First, we sought to ensure that the  in vitro  methodology resulted in the differentiation of bone marrow cells into phenotypically immunosuppressive macrophages reflective of human gTAMs utilizing a multimodal approach including CyTOF, bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), and functional assays. Canonically polarized M1 and M2 macrophage controls were utilized as controls for the gTAMs ( Figure 1A ). By day 7 in culture, gTAMs appeared as viable cells with heterogeneous morphology attributed to polarized macrophages, including elongation, circularity, and granularity 39  ( Figure 1B ). Morphological heterogeneity was observed between the different gTAMs, with U251 gTAMs exhibiting greater elongation, likely reflecting cell line-specific effects on their polarization. On day 7, the cells were non-enzymatically lifted and the myeloid phenotype of the gTAMs was assessed by CyTOF and flow cytometry. Initial unsupervised CyTOF clustering highlights the heterogeneity and diversity of the different gTAMs compared to the M1 and M2 controls ( Figure 1C ).
L48:To confirm the differentiation of bone marrow-derived monocytes into macrophages, we first performed RNAseq of monocytes isolated from the bone marrow of healthy mice and compared the expression of monocytic and macrophage-related genes to that of GL261 gTAMs, U87 gTAMs, CT2A gTAMs, and U251 gTAMs ( Figure 2A ). Genes associated with the monocyte lineage and trafficking, including  Ly6c1, Ccr2, Csf1r  ( CD115 ), and  Cx3cr1 , were upregulated in the bone marrow-derived monocytes compared to the gTAMs. Furthermore, genes associated with macrophage differentiation, including  Cd68  and  Adgre1  (F4/80), were upregulated in the gTAMs. Additionally, genes associated with TAM phenotypes, including  Hmox1, Spp1, Trem2 , and  Adam8  were upregulated in the different gTAMs ( Figure 2A ). To validate the gene expression data, we performed immunophenotyping by both flow cytometry and CyTOF. Flow cytometry of the gTAMs confirmed that a canonical murine bone marrow-derived macrophage phenotype (CD45 high  CD11b+ F4/80+) was generated in culture ( Figure 2B ). 40 , 41  Further, CyTOF confirmed gTAMs to be CD45 high  CD11b+ F4/80+  with low expression of the monocytic markers Ly6C, CCR2, and CX3CR1 and the neutrophil marker Ly6G ( Figure 2C , Supplemental Figure 2). Together, these results indicate that bone marrow-derived monocytes successfully differentiate into macrophages  in vitro , reflecting  in vivo  TAM phenotypes.

L50:Recent advancements in single-cell technologies have demonstrated that TAMs isolated from GBM tumors are a heterogeneous population of macrophages that exist on a spectrum, rather than a canonical M1 or M2 phenotype, skewed towards an immunosuppressive phenotype. 13 , 19 , 20  To assess the phenotypes of the different  in vitro  generated gTAMs, we compared the expression of genes implicated in myeloid activation or immunosuppression against bone marrow-derived monocytes via RNAseq ( Figure 3A ). Compared to monocytes, gTAMs expressed variable but consistently higher levels of immunosuppressive/M2-like genes, while expressing lower levels of myeloid activation/M1-like genes. The gTAMs expressed high levels of immunosuppressive genes, including  Mrc1  ( CD206 ),  Arg1 ,  Msr1 ,  Socs2 ,  Chil3 ,  Timp1 ,  Retnla , and  Vsig4 . The gTAMs exhibited lower gene expression of molecules associated with myeloid activation compared to monocytes overall, although not exclusively. The gTAMs expressed certain genes implicated in myeloid activation, including  CD80 ,  Nos2 ,  Socs3 , and  Il6  ( Figure 3A ). The variable expression of genes associated with the TGFβ family, including  Tgfb1 ,  Tgfb2 ,  Tgfb3 , and  Inhba , in gTAMs further suggests complexity in transcriptional pathways. Interestingly, increased expression of genes associated with angiogenesis, including  Vegfa ,  Vegfb , and  Ang2,  were observed in the gTAMs, which is consistent with observations in GBM TAMs  in vivo  ( Figure 3A ). 42

L68:The crosstalk between glioma cells and TAMs likely involves a bidirectional interaction in which each cell type actively influences the phenotype and function of the other, contributing to tumor progression and immune modulation within the tumor microenvironment. To assess this phenomenon, we harvested the supernatant, or macrophage-conditioned medium (MCM), of GL261 gTAMs, M1 macrophages, and M2 macrophages on day 7 of culture, hereby referred to as GL261_gTAMs_MCM, M1_MCM, and M2_MCM, respectively. We cultured the GL261 cell line in control media (bone marrow cells cultured in Hibernate media with M-CSF for 7 d), GL261_gTAMs_MCM, M1_MCM, or M2_MCM for 24 h and assessed the proliferation rate of the glioma cells via the EdU incorporation assay ( Figure 7A ). The GL261_gTAMs_MCM significantly increased the proliferation rate of the GL261 cell line compared to control media or M1_MCM ( Figure 7B,C ). We conducted this experiment with the U251 and CT2A cell lines and observed that gTAMs_MCM significantly increased the proliferation of the corresponding glioma cell lines ( Figure 7C ). Together, these results indicate the ability of gTAMs to enhance the growth of tumor cells. We further investigated the complex crosstalk of the glioma TME, as summarized ( Figure 8 ).

L72:Treatments for GBM have been stagnant for decades, and the standard of care remains maximal safe resection followed by chemotherapy and radiation. Promising new therapeutics, particularly checkpoint inhibitors, have failed to elicit a therapeutic response in GBM for a variety of reasons, including myeloid-associated resistance. 4  A plethora of myeloid-targeting strategies, such as CSF1R and CCR2 inhibitors, have demonstrated survival benefits in preclinical studies but have translated poorly to the clinic. 8 , 12  Thus, a deeper understanding of the biology and plasticity of TAMs in GBM and how to best target these cells for depletion or repolarization is needed. Here, we developed and investigated a novel methodology for generating gTAMs utilizing commercially available human and murine cell lines, cytokines, and healthy mouse BMDM for application in cancer immunology research. We employed a rigorous, multimodal approach to gTAMs phenotyping including flow cytometry, CyTOF, RNA-seq, Luminex, and functional assays. Our results establish gTAMs as heterogeneous, dynamic immune cells with immunosuppressive functionality while also retaining some aspects of an activated macrophage. Further, this phenotype was relatively consistent between gTAMs educated by the four different glioma cell lines, regardless of their origin from human or mouse. The gTAMs phenotype reflects the complexity of human GBM-associated TAMs that have been the topic of recent debate in the literature and offers novel insight into what specific molecules the gTAMs express and secrete. 1 , 19 , 21 , 22
L76:Advancements in single-cell and spatial technologies have enabled researchers to elucidate novel molecules imperative to our understanding of TAMs in GBM. For example, the gTAMs in this study demonstrated expression of Trem2, Hmox1, CD74, and Spp1, all of which have been identified on myeloid cells in GBM tumors and implicated in various protumoral functions. 16 , 30 , 42 , 70  Importantly, gTAMs are bone marrow-derived macrophages and may better represent the phenotype and behavior of monocyte-derived infiltrating macrophages in brain tumors without requiring the notoriously imperfect separation of resident microglia. Recently, Miller et al reported four principal immunomodulatory programs associated with myeloid cells in glioma including systemic inflammatory, microglial inflammatory, complement immunosuppressive, and scavenger immunosuppressive. 7  We compared the expression patterns of the genes for each program against our gTAMs and found high similarity for the scavenger and complement immunosuppressive programs (Supplemental Figure 3). Interestingly, Miller et al. reported that myeloid cells expressing the scavenger and complement immunosuppressive programs are associated with a higher glioma grade and higher Treg infiltration in human samples. 7
