L1:Macrophage-derived amphiregulin induces myofibroblast transition in adipogenic lineage precursors near  Staphylococcus aureus  abscess in bone marrow
L8:The formation of  Staphylococcus aureus  ( S. aureus ) abscesses is a well-established determinant of persistent skeletal infections, yet the mechanisms underlying bacterial persistence remain elusive. Here, we demonstrate that bone marrow adiponectin-positive ( Adipoq + ) precursors are mobilized to surround  S. aureus  abscesses and undergo myofibroblast differentiation. This phenotypic transition induces vascular constriction, thereby impairing local perfusion and impeding effective bacterial clearance. Mechanistically, macrophage-derived amphiregulin (AREG) activates EGFR signaling on  Adipoq +  cells, triggering the mTOR/YAP pathway to drive their myofibroblast transition. Importantly, genetic ablation of  Adipoq +  cells, cell-specific deletion of the AREG/EGFR axis, or pharmacological inhibition of EGFR/mTOR signaling effectively alleviates fibrosis, restores vascular perfusion and antibiotic delivery, and promotes bacterial eradication from abscesses. Our findings implicate a macrophage- Adipoq +  cell regulatory axis that sustains  S. aureus  persistence in osteomyelitis and identify therapeutic targeting of this axis as a strategy to enhance antibiotic efficacy against  S. aureus  skeletal infections.

L13:The persistence of  S. aureus  in skeletal infections is driven by several factors, such as biofilms associated with implants, abscess formation, intracellular infections, and invasion into the osteocyte lacuno-canalicular network 12 . Recent clinical data highlight that local abscess formation significantly escalates the risk of treatment failure in  S. aureus  bone infections 13 . These deep-tissue  S. aureus  abscess communities, within bone marrow, not only inhibit the bactericidal activity of nearby neutrophils and macrophages 14 , 15 , but also facilitate spread and creation of new infection sites, becoming a sustaining source of chronic bone infection 16 , 17 . Although the cellular composition of  S. aureus  abscesses within the bone marrow is well documented 1 , 12 , 18 , 19 , the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying antibiotic failure in these  S. aureus  abscess communities remain poorly understood.
L14:Here, we report that adiponectin-positive ( Adipoq + ) cells, a type of marrow adipogenic lineage precursors (MALPs) in bone marrow, which predominantly regulate bone marrow homeostasis and support macrophage development, osteoclastogenesis, and hematopoietic stem cell regeneration 20 – 22 , are important for myofibroblast formation adjacent to  S. aureus  abscesses. This, in turn, significantly restricts blood perfusion to the infected areas. Moreover, we have identified that amphiregulin (AREG), secreted by bone marrow macrophages, is pivotal in initiating the myofibroblast transition in  Adipoq +  cells by stimulating the EGFR/mTOR/YAP signaling pathway. Our investigations further show that targeted deletion of AREG in bone marrow macrophages, ablation of EGFR in  Adipoq +  cells, or pharmacological inhibition of EGFR/mTOR signaling effectively decreases myofibroblast development, enhances vascular perfusion and antibiotics delivery, and subsequently diminishes bacterial burden and mitigates bone destruction in  S. aureus -induced osteomyelitis. These findings elucidate critical mechanisms promoting  staphylococcal  abscess pathogenesis during osteomyelitis progression, providing a foundation for developing targeted antifibrotic therapies that may potentiate antibiotic efficacy against  S. aureus  infections in the skeleton.

L27:Given that  Adipoq +  cells are an important niche component for bone marrow hematopoiesis 29 , 30 , genetic ablation of them or their phenotype alterations under  S. aureus  infection could potentially alter the local effector immune response. Consequently, we performed flow cytometry analyses to quantify the immune cell populations and ROS levels in neutrophils and macrophages from both DT-treated  AdipoqCre-iDTR  mice and DT-treated  iDTR  control mice, with or without  S. aureus  osteomyelitis in the femurs. Whether in non-infected or infected states,  Adipoq +  cell depletion did not alter the proportion of neutrophils, macrophages, and dendritic cells, but it induced a moderate reduction in monocytes under  S. aureus -infected conditions (Fig.  S7A –F ). Notably, in the  S. aureus  osteomyelitis model, ablation of  Adipoq +  cells reduced both T and B cell populations (Fig.  S8A – D ), while neutrophil and macrophage ROS production remained unaffected (Fig.  S9A – D ). These findings imply that mechanisms other than localized immune responses likely contribute to the enhanced bacterial clearance observed after  Adipoq +  cell ablation.
L28:Next, we tested whether  S. aureus  infection directly triggers myofibroblastic transition in preadipocytes. Unexpectedly,  S. aureus  infection had no effect on the mRNA levels of myofibroblast marker genes ( Acta2 ,  Tagln ,  Col-I ,  Lox  and  Loxl2 ) in 3T3-L1 adipocyte precursor cell line (Fig.  S10A ). These results were validated in primary mouse bone marrow preadipocytes (BMpreA), with over 90% of differentiated cells expressing characteristic preadipocyte markers adiponectin and stem cell factor (SCF) (Fig.  S10B – D ). Furthermore, we didn’t observe the intracellular colocalization of  S. aureus  in  Adipoq +  cells in vivo (Fig.  S10E ). Collectively, these findings indicate that the myofibroblast transition of  Adipoq +  cells in  S. aureus -induced osteomyelitis occurs indirectly, likely mediated by abscess microenvironment factors rather than a direct influence of bacterial infection.

L43:Our recent findings demonstrate that EGFR signaling hyperactivation in BMDMs compromises their antimicrobial activity, while pharmacological inhibition with erlotinib effectively reduced bacterial burden and improved bone morphometry in  S. aureus  osteomyelitis 36 . Building upon these findings, we investigated whether pharmacological EGFR blockade could attenuate myofibroblast transition of  Adipoq +  cells and enhance abscess vascularization. Similar to erlotinib, treatment with gefitinib (another EGFR inhibitor) significantly lowered bacterial loads in infected femurs and implants while improving both cortical and trabecular bone microstructure (Fig.  S23A – K ). Importantly, the gefitinib-treated group exhibited markedly reduced myofibroblast transition, as evidenced by decreased Adiponectin + α-SMA +  and Adiponectin + Col-I +  double-positive cells surrounding  S. aureus  abscess (Fig.  S23L – N ). Moreover, gefitinib treatment enhanced antibiotic delivery, evidenced by greater distribution of FITC-gentamicin in abscesses (Fig.  S23O ,  P ). These findings collectively suggest that combining EGFR inhibitors with antibiotics may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for  S. aureus  osteomyelitis.
