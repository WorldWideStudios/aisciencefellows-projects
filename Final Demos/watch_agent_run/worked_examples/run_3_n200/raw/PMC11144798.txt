L1:Protocol for extracting and isolating porcine bone-marrow-derived macrophages from ribs
L6:Due to anatomical and biological similarities with humans, pigs are increasingly used for inflammation- and immune-related studies in biomedical research, including the field of osteonecrosis and osteoimmunology. Here, we present a protocol for rib extraction, isolation of the bone marrow by centrifugation, and processing to obtain bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs). Then, we describe the procedures of  in vitro  experiments to evaluate the cell phenotype.
L11:The protocol below describes the specific steps to obtain BMDM from porcine ribs. We use porcine BMDMs to investigate the effects of necrotic bone on macrophages as we previously found that necrotic bone from a porcine model of ischemic osteonecrosis of the femoral head stimulates macrophage-inflammatory responses through Toll-like receptor 4. 2  Moreover, damage associated molecular patterns from necrotic femoral heads of a porcine model of ischemic osteonecrosis inhibit osteogenesis and promote fibrogenesis. 3  The BMDMs isolated from the protocol are used to further investigate the role of macrophages in immune response and repair process following ischemic osteonecrosis of the femoral head in the porcine model.
L30:This initial step prepares the porcine chest region for rib dissection. The rib retrieval is performed in an animal operating room. To minimize the risk of contamination, it is necessary to use sterile techniques. 1. Cover the animal with sterile drapes and cut out a triangle of 10–15 cm window to expose the skin for rib cage dissection ( Figure 1 ). Figure 1 Porcine ribs extraction The illustration describes steps 1–10 for rib extraction and preparation. 2. Remove the skin and the sternum with a scalpel and identify the seven ribs to be dissected on each side (generally from 4 th  to 10 th  ribs from each side). 3. Cut and remove the cartilaginous rib from the osteochondral junction of each rib. 4. Peel the periosteum along each rib from the anterior to the posterior region of the rib using an elevator (Tool #1,  Figure 1 ). 5. Separate the periosteum around each rib towards the spine. 6. Cut the ribs as far posterior (towards the spine) as possible using a bone cutter (Tool #2,  Figure 1 ). 7. Keep the cut ribs in a sterilized bowl for temporary storage (Tool #3,  Figure 1 ). Note:  Fill saline into the bowl to keep the ribs moist. 8. After harvesting the ribs from both sides, remove all remaining soft tissue on the ribs using a bone curette (Tool #4,  Figure 1 ). 9. Cut the ribs into 1–1.5 cm pieces using the bone cutter (Tool #2 and 5,  Figure 1 ). 10. Load rib pieces longitudinally in 15 mL sterile centrifuge tubes for bone marrow cell isolation ( Figure 1 ).

L34:This step describes the extraction of bone marrow (BM) by a centrifugation step, and the isolation of monocytes using Ficoll separation. The isolated monocytes can be used for  in vitro  experiments or stored for future studies ( Figure 2 ). 11. Weigh the 15 mL tubes with rib pieces to create the necessary balance for centrifugation. 12. Centrifuge at room temperature (RT) at 2000 rpm for 30 s, then 3000 rpm for 1 min. 13. Transfer rib pieces from 15 mL tubes into a separate 50 mL tube using sterile tweezers.  Troubleshooting 1 . 14. Add 3 mL of PBE into the 15 mL tubes to resuspend the bone marrow via gentle pipetting. 15. Merge the BM suspension into 50 mL tubes. 16. Pass the BM suspension through 500 μm and 100 μm strainers to remove the tissue debris and bone particles. Note:  Pipetting can be applied to avoid the aggregation of tissue debris and facilitate the passage of BM suspension through the strainer. Note:  To collect as many cells as possible, all tubes are washed with 5–10 mL of PBE. The wash medium is passed through a 100 μm strainer and merged into the BM cell suspension. 17. Perform a Ficoll centrifuge step to separate the monocytes from the other cells.  Troubleshooting 2 . a. Add 20 mL of Ficoll Paque Plus into 50 mL tubes. b. Gently transfer 25 mL of BM cell suspension into the Ficoll tube on top. c. Centrifuge for 30 min at 1350 rpm and 20°C. d. Collect the buffy coat using a pipette into new 50 mL tubes. e. Centrifuge for 10 min at 1000 rpm and 15°C to remove the debris and Ficoll Plaque solution. 18. Add ACK lysis buffer to remove the red blood cells. a. Remove the supernatant and resuspend the pellet using 20 mL ACK lysis buffer. b. Incubate the cells for 3 min at RT. c. Centrifuge for 5 min at 1200 rpm and 15°C. d. Aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in 10 mL of PBE. Note:  If a large amount of debris is visible inside the tube, filter through a 100 μm strainer one more time. 19. Count the cells with Trypan Blue to check cell number and viability.  Troubleshooting 3 . L35:BMDM selection, culture, and differentiation
L37:Figure 3 Purification and stimulation of BMDM The Illustration describes steps 20–25 for macrophage purification and macrophage stimulations.
L38:To obtain a high macrophage yield, we remove the non-adhered cells 1 h after the cell seeding ( Figure 3 ). We also culture the cells for four days, allowing the macrophage maturation. Additionally, we add chemical stimulants into the cell culture medium to induce macrophage differentiation. 20. Seed 4×10 7  cells into 6-well petri dishes with 2 mL/well of macrophage culture medium. 21. Culture the cells in a cell culture incubator for 1 h. 22. Change the macrophage culture medium to remove the floating cells. a. Remove the supernatant medium by using an aspirator. b. Wash the culture dishes using 2 mL/well of PBS. 23. Add 2 mL of fresh macrophage culture medium and keep the cells in a cell culture incubator for 4 days to obtain matured macrophages.  Troubleshooting 4 . 24. Change the macrophage culture medium and supply it with chemical stimulants for macrophage stimulations. Note:  To induce M (LPS+IFNγ) and M (IL-4+IL-13) macrophages, the macrophage culture medium is supplied with 100 ng/mL LPS + 20 ng/mL IFNγ and 20 ng/mL IL-4 + 20 ng/mL IL-13, respectively. The cells without stimulants are used as a control. Note:  Reconstitute the stimulants before the treatment by adding PBS inside each vial. 25. Culture the cells in a cell culture incubator for another 24 h. L49:The BMDM had different sizes and morphologies (round and elongated shapes), as shown in  Figure 6 A. The FACS analyses showed that the BMDM had a high CD14 in all groups, and 98.8% of cells were CD14-positive, indicating a high purity of macrophages ( Figure 6 B). The LPS+IFNγ stimulated macrophages showed an increased CD80/86 population (80.9%) and expression intensity (MFI, 981 ± 91) compared to the non-stimulated macrophages (MFI, 643 ± 83,  p  = 0.0149) and to the M (IL-4+IL13) macrophages (279 ± 90,  p  = 0.0004,  Figure 6 B). On the other hand, the M (IL-4+IL-13) macrophages showed an increased SWC9 population (97.6%) and expression intensity (MFI, 3321 ± 383) compared to the non-stimulated macrophages (MFI, 2167 ± 266,  p  = 0.0011) and to the M (LPS+IFNγ) macrophages (MFI, 958 ± 89,  p  < 0.0001). L52:The present protocol for porcine BMDM extraction and isolation provides an alternative method to bone marrow cell extraction using needle aspiration or flushing the long bones, which have been described previously. 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12  The number of BM cells obtained by centrifuging the cut pieces of ribs was relatively higher compared to the method obtained from long bones. The number of BM cells obtained was also consistent and reproducible. Furthermore, using the centrifuge extraction method, the amount of time necessary to extract the BM cells was drastically reduced, and dilution of bone marrow cells by flushing with PBS or saline solution was avoided.
L54:One limitation is the specimen transportation. We collected the rib pieces in the animal facility, which is a 5-min walk from the facility for cell processing and culturing. The concern comes from the time and temperature change during the transportation of the specimens, which may affect the cell viability and marrow clotting. It’s important to avoid drastic changes in temperature. For this reason, transporting bone marrow on ice is not recommended. Also, during the extraction process PBE must be placed in RT for at least 30 min before starting the bone marrow extraction and cell isolation. The different ages and weights of the donor animals are additional limitations because different ages and related pig weights could affect the cell yield based on the variation of acquired bone marrow.
