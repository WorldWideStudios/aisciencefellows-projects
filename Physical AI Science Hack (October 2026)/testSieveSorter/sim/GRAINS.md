# Grains

Every grain kind in `grains.py`: what it is, the numbers the sim uses and where they come from. The team's pantry photo of 2026-10-25 (a divided tray, one phone screenshot) added six kinds to the original four. White rice came last, after the review: the user pointed out that the sim's `rice` isn't the white rice in the photo. Units: mm, g, s. Density is in g/mm³ (1.45e-3 = 1.45 g/cm³). A review the same day changed couscous, the wild-rice length spread and three swatch colours; [Review of 2026-10-25](#review-of-2026-10-25) lists what was changed and what was not.

**The first four kinds are frozen.** rice, quinoa, chickpea and pasta are the kinds the design optimizer's leaderboard was run with. Their numbers and code paths are unchanged, so the same rng state still gives byte-identical XML and dims (checked over 38,502 draws against the committed `grains.py`, and over 9,114 more after `load_measured()` on a synthetic CSV). Changes to them are only suggested, in [Suggested changes](#suggested-changes-to-the-frozen-kinds-not-applied).

**None of this is calibrated.** Sizes come from the literature, with the photo used to identify each grain, give its colour, and check its size. The photo has no ruler. Its scale (about 0.20 mm/px) assumes the ditalini are 6.5 mm across, so its absolute sizes are good to about ±10%. Chia, at a well-sourced 2.12 × 1.31 mm [12], is a second scale for the small grains. Every thickness is an assumption, because an overhead photo can't show it. Calipers and a balance (BENCH.md step 3) replace all of it: once `bench/grain_dims.csv` has 5 rows of a kind, `load_measured()` takes that kind's mean size, spread and density from them.

## All kinds

L × W × T is length × width × thickness, sorted largest first. Spread is the relative SD of each dimension (one value means all three), clipped to the range in brackets. Mass is density × the shape's volume at the mean size. "Hole" is the smallest square hole the mean grain passes at its best tilt (`common.ellipse_fits`), and "slot" is its thickness, the smallest gap it passes. Bracketed numbers are [sources](#sources).

### Per kind

- **white_rice.** The photo's white rice, which the frozen `rice` doesn't match. Isolated grains in the photo measure 7.2 ± 0.3 mm long (33–37.5 px on 5 grains), inside the IRRI long class [24]; `rice` is 6.5 mm at 16.8 mg. This kind takes Riceland's US long-grain milled rice [19] (Diamond 7.23 × 2.12 × 1.72 mm at 21.6 mg; Ozark 7.45 × 2.14 × 1.71 mm at 22.1 mg), rounded to the photo's 7.2 mm. That is Suggested change 1 applied as a new kind instead of to `rice`. The density, 1.56e-3, is Diamond's mass over its ellipsoid volume, the mass-matched convention of the other new kinds, so the mean grain weighs 21.0 mg. The width is what matters at 2 mm. Its smallest hole is 1.91 mm at the mean size, against 1.86 mm for `rice`, and at the default 7% spread a 2 mm square passes 83% of grains in principle (`rice`: 95%), 45% at 1.9 mm and 98% at 2.1 mm (cross-section rule, 4,000 draws). The photo shows no short-grain white or brown rice, so neither is built; the short grains in the photo are `black_rice`.
- **brown_rice.** The literature puts US long-grain brown rice at 7.2–7.7 × 2.25–2.54 × 1.95–2.02 mm [1]. That comes from a search summary of the abstract, because the paper couldn't be opened. California long grains run 7.8–8.0 × 2.16–2.22 mm, at 21.6–23.5 g per 1000 kernels [2]. In the photo the brown rice is only about 1.27× as long as the black ovals, so the length is set to 7.2 mm. If the bag is a California long grain, use 7.9. The width, 2.2 mm, is [2]'s. The thickness, 1.85 mm, is [1]'s T/W of 0.84 applied to that width, and the sources disagree around it:
  - [1]'s direct thicknesses are 1.95–2.02 mm, but at widths of 2.25–2.54 mm.
  - Milled US long grains are 1.71–1.72 mm thick [19]. Brown rice is 0.08 mm wider than milled in [2] (2.16 against 2.08 mm for L-208). The same bran margin on the thickness gives about 1.80 mm.
  - [3]'s 12 Kashmir cultivars are 1.78–2.20 mm thick (mean 1.99), but they are short, bold temperate grains, not long grains.

  The spreads 4.7% (L) and 7.2% (W) are the CVs of [4]'s brown-rice control (6.76 ± 0.32 × 2.65 ± 0.19 mm, n = 6), and 5% for T is an assumption. Its 21 mg mass agrees with true density (mean 1.355 g/cm³ over 12 cultivars [3]) times the ellipsoid volume (20.8 mg). Brown rice sits right at the 2 mm screen, under both rules:
  - **Square hole.** Its smallest hole at the mean size is 2.03 mm, and 67.5% of drawn grains need a hole between 1.9 and 2.1 mm. A 2 mm square passes 36% of them in principle, 8% if the holes print at 1.9 mm, and 75% at 2.1 mm. The ±0.1 mm print error is assumed.
  - **2 mm slot.** The slot is decided by the thickness. At the sim's spreads it passes 99% at T = 1.80, 95% at 1.85, 62% at 1.97 and 56% at [1]'s midpoint (2.40 × 1.98).

  Until calipers give W and T, brown_rice results on a 2 mm screen are not sorting results.
- **black_rice.** Black rice and red rice are one kind, because their sizes match within the photo's scale (26.4 × 13.9 px against 26.8 × 13.5 px) and the photo can't cleanly split their colours. The literature has short-grain black japonicas at 5.15 × 2.61 mm, 17.0 g per 1000 kernels, and 5.74 × 2.51 mm, 16.2 g [5]. Black Manipur rice is 6.3 × 2.5 × 1.9 mm, 18 g [6]. For red, [3] has one red cultivar, Kaw Quder ("deep red colored" kernels): 5.72 × 3.25 × 2.11 mm, 26.3 g per 1000 kernels, true density 1.313 g/cm³. With the next two by redness (a*), Shel Kew and Zag, which [3] doesn't call red, the range is 5.4–5.9 × 2.8–3.3 × 1.9–2.1 mm, 22–26 g and 1.28–1.35 g/cm³. Black rice has the lowest density of four pigmented rices [7], which gives no number. The dims are the black values, moved a third of the way toward these three. The photo's half-maximum width reads wider still (3.05 mm), and three grains that look on edge hint at a thickness of 2.5 mm. That matters: at T = 1.95 the kind is right at a 2 mm gap. A "Black Japonica" blend is sold as about 75% mahogany and 25% black [8]. The photo shows about a third red, so the blend on the bench may not be that one. Use rgba 0.46 0.31 0.26 for the red shade if a vision study needs it.
- **wild_rice.** Cultivated *Zizania palustris* kernels (dehulled, unmilled) measure 11.1 × 1.92 mm, 32.6 g per 1000 kernels, for KC-755. Commercial samples run 8.3–11.3 × 1.60–2.13 mm, 22–35 mg each [9]. Whole-looking kernels in the photo are about 8–12.4 × 1.5–2.1 mm. With chia as the scale, their half-maximum width is 1.19–1.28× chia's, about 1.6–1.7 mm (the sim's capsule is 1.30×). The width sources disagree:
  - [4]'s three cultivar classes are all 2.50–2.59 mm wide (short class 9.49 ± 1.63 × 2.53 ± 0.39 mm). That is about 37% above the sim and too wide to pass a 2 mm square, so they were not used.
  - The Whole Grains Council's grades run narrower. Grade A is wider than 1.49 mm, grade B is 1.19–1.49 mm and grade C narrower, with 5–20% broken [10]. A grade-B bench wild rice would sit below the sim's 1.72 mm capsule diameter.

  The capsule is round, so make() gives it the diameter √(W·T): a round section of the same area passes square holes at 1.72 mm, against 1.73 mm for the true W × T ellipse. Its dims report width = thickness = that diameter, so a slot rule sees 1.72 mm where the real thickness is about 1.6. Because make() takes the geometric mean of independent W and T draws, the diameter's spread is 5.7%, not 8%. The length spread is 17%, the CV of [4]'s short class (1.63 / 9.49, n = 6). [4]'s other rows give 7–15%, and a class cut at 10 mm understates the whole population. Eight isolated needles in the photo vary more (CV 0.20), but some of them may be broken. Clipped at ±30% (7–13 mm), the realised length CV is 0.16.

  Wild rice is the longest grain here but has the smallest cross-section of the rice family, so it can drop end-first through holes that hold back white rice. It also bridges 5 mm holes. To drop end-first through a 2 mm hole in the 1.4 mm thick 2 mm screen, the 1.72 mm rod has to be within about 10° of vertical along a hole side, or 30° along the diagonal. That is an estimate, D / cos a + h tan a ≤ s, and it ignores the 8° relief taper.
- **chia.** The size and the 1.323 g per 1000 seeds are Ixtaina et al.'s dark seeds [11], confirmed by [12] (2.12 × 1.31 × 0.82 mm, 1.384 g) and [13] (1.23–1.35 g). The density is mass-matched: 1.323 mg over the ellipsoid's 1.18 mm³. That is the convention `load_measured()` uses. It sits at the top of the measured true densities, 0.93–1.11 g/cm³ [11] [13], because an ellipsoid is smaller than the seed. White seeds are 2.15 × 1.40 × 0.83 mm, within 2–6% of the dark ones [11]. Per-dimension SDs of 2–5% appear only in a search summary of [14], whose authors couldn't be confirmed. Per grain at half maximum, the photo's chia have an equivalent-diameter CV of 0.06–0.07, an upper bound at 5 px/mm (the sim's is 0.04), so 5.5% per dimension is kept. Chia exudes sticky mucilage within minutes when wet. `spray.py` can't model that, so a wet chia run means nothing.
- **couscous.** Couscous is the call for the pale grains in the middle-right compartment, on colour and plan shape:
  - **Colour.** They are semolina-yellow: against the tray the pile reads about 20 b* yellower, where the brown rice reads 13.
  - **Plan shape.** They are oval, not round. Isolated grains measure 10.4 × 7.8 px at half maximum (n = 12, minor/major 0.75), against chia's 9.5 × 6.5 px (n = 11, 0.68 where the true ratio is 0.62 [12]), so the photo preserves aspect ratio at this size. Quinoa (2.04 × 2.03 mm [18]) and proso millet (sphericity 0.93–0.96 [23]) are round in plan view. Amaranth (about 1.39 × 1.29 mm [18]) is smaller than chia, and these grains are larger.

  Two earlier arguments are dropped. Their size spread is wider than chia's (per-grain CV 0.19–0.21 against 0.06–0.07; an earlier global-threshold measurement gave 0.39–0.41, which the per-grain method doesn't support), but sieved commercial couscous can be as uniform as seeds: [17]'s 81 grains have an equivalent-diameter CV of 0.086. Classical couscous is also smooth and round ("spherical shape with regular surfaces", sphericity 0.844 [15]), not lumpy. The soak test in the open questions settles the ID.

  **Size.** With chia as the scale (1.31 mm wide [12]), the grains' half-maximum minor axis is 1.19–1.27× chia's, so W ≈ 1.57–1.68 mm. Their major axis is 1.09–1.24× chia's, so L ≈ 2.3–2.6 mm (both at half-maximum fractions of 0.5 and 0.4). At the ditalini scale of 0.20 mm/px, the same grains are 2.1–2.4 × 1.56–1.76 mm. The earlier 1.75 × 1.3 mm came from a global threshold, which undersizes these low-contrast grains (about 10 b* units of contrast, against 42 L* units for chia). Scattered grains may be biased large, because big ones roll further. Industrial couscous has d50 1.60 mm, span 0.56 and compactness 0.81, made from semolina of 1.415–1.453 g/cm³ at 0.10 g/g water [15]. The Codex standard allows 0.63–2.0 mm [16].

  So W = 1.6 mm. L = W / 0.744 = 2.15 mm and T = 0.82 W = 1.3 mm, using the mean W/L and T/W of [17]'s micro-CT scan (convexity 0.77). [17] doesn't state the grade, and its grains are much smaller, with mean axes of 0.94 × 0.69 × 0.56 mm. The plan-view equivalent diameter, 1.85 mm, is 1.11× chia's, inside the photo's 1.02–1.15×. The density is derived as 0.81 × 1.453 × 1.10 = 1.29 g/cm³. That gives 3.0 mg at the mean size and 3.5 mg as the mean per grain, against 1.6 mg before the review.

  **Spread.** [15]'s span of 0.56 is σ/d50 ≈ 0.22 for a normal distribution (span = 2.56 σ/d50). The photo's isolated grains give 0.19–0.21. In `make()` one draw (CV 22%) sets the width and scales the whole grain, and the other two are shape noise on L/W (15%) and T/W (10.5%), [17]'s per-grain CVs. This is `SIZE_LINKED` in `grains.py`. The earlier three independent draws averaged out, which left a smallest-hole CV of only 0.13. Over 20,000 grains the realised CVs are now W 0.21, L 0.25, T 0.23, W/L 0.14 and T/W 0.10. The smallest square hole has a mean of 1.46 mm, CV 0.21 and p5–p95 0.93–1.98 mm. The ±40% clip still lets 4% of grains need a hole above 2 mm, outside the Codex range. That is in line with [15]: if its distribution is symmetric, its span puts d90 at about 2.05 mm.

  **Against chia,** a 1.2 mm square hole passes 99% of sim chia and 22% of sim couscous, and a 1.15 mm hole passes 88% and 17%. At this spread the two can't be cleanly separated by hole size. At [17]'s spread (CV 0.09) they would separate at about 1.2 mm.

  If the packet says quinoa, delete this kind and use `quinoa` with the white colour (Suggested changes).

## Suggested changes to the frozen kinds (not applied)

Any of these would change optimizer results, so they wait for the leaderboard's next rebuild and for calipers.

1. **rice: longer, a little wider, heavier.** Riceland's measurements of US long-grain milled rice are 7.23 × 2.12 × 1.72 mm at 21.6 mg (Diamond) and 7.45 × 2.14 × 1.71 mm at 22.1 mg (Ozark) [19]. The photo reads 6.6–7.8 × 1.8–2.4 mm. The sim's 6.5 × 2.0 × 1.7 mm at 16.8 mg is 10–13% short, 6% narrow and 22–24% light. The width is what matters: with the sim's 7% spread, the 2 mm square screen passes 93% of sim rice and only 72–75% of rice at Diamond or Ozark dims (cross-section rule, Monte Carlo). Suggested: (7.3, 2.1, 1.7) with a mass-matched density of 1.55e-3, but only if calipers give a mean width of 2.05 mm or more. Until then, `white_rice` carries nearly these numbers as its own kind, so a study can use the photo's rice without touching `rice`.
2. **pasta: lighter, and maybe longer.** The 8 boxes overlap at their inner corners, so the tube is 155 mm³ (225 mg). A round 6.5 / 4.1 mm tube 6.0 mm long is 174 mg, and Barilla's Ditali n.45 (6.15 mm across, 6.0 long, 1.10 wall) [20] would be 152 mg. The fix is a box half-width of `r_mid * tan(pi/8)` instead of `r_out * tan(pi/8)`, or a density from weighed pieces. `load_measured()` never fits the pasta density, even when masses are given. On length the photo is split. One reading puts it at 7.5 mm (6.7–8.4) with a wall of 1.3–1.5 mm. Another has the tubes about as long as wide (6–7 mm) with a wall of 1.2–1.3. Calipers decide. A length of 7.5 would raise the sorted thickness from 6.0 to 6.5 mm, which matters for any gap near 6 mm. The photo's pasta is paler than the sim: about 0.85 0.65 0.37. The bench ditalini are not Italian 5 mm ditalini (Barilla's Ditalini 43/33: 5.0 mm across, 5.5 long, 1.0 wall [20]). Those would jam a 5 mm screen, passing it in principle 52% of the time.
3. **quinoa: heavier.** The geometry agrees with [18] (2.04 × 2.03 × 1.06 mm). [18]'s 3.01 g per 1000 seeds over that ellipsoid gives 1.31e-3, against the sim's 1.20e-3. If the pale grains turn out to be white quinoa, change the colour to ivory (about 0.90 0.86 0.74). With chia as the scale they read 2.3–2.6 × 1.6–1.7 mm and oval (minor/major about 0.72–0.75), not 2.0 mm round discs, which is one more reason to think they aren't quinoa.
4. **chickpea:** not in the photo, nothing to compare.
5. **Guard the frozen kinds in `load_measured()`.** It runs at import. As soon as `grain_dims.csv` holds 5 rows of `rice` or `pasta`, those numbers silently replace SIZES for every run, the optimizer included. Until the leaderboard is rebuilt, either record the bench white rice as `white_rice` (its own kind, so the rows fit it and leave `rice` alone) and the ditalini under a name that isn't in SIZES, such as `pasta_bench`, which `load_measured()` ignores, or skip the `FROZEN` kinds unless a flag is set. The code is unchanged here, because the guard would change what a frozen kind does.
6. **Minor, `common.ellipse_fits` docstring:** at its nominal size, 2.0 × 1.7 mm rice fits a 2 mm square lying flat. Only grains wider than 2.0 mm need the tilt.

## Suggested changes to shared code (not applied)

The sieve inserts and the ideal-zone rule are shared with the optimizer, so these are only written down.

1. **Sieve insert margins.** `cad/sieve_insert.py` fits whole holes with a bar at each edge (`nx = (window - bar) // pitch`). That leaves a solid strip between the outer hole row and the window wall that is wider than a bar. On the 5 mm insert the strip is 3.20 mm wide along the sides that run with the stroke and 2.67 mm at the ends, against a 1.2 mm bar. On the 2 mm insert it is 2.10 and 1.57 mm. With a pure along-tray stroke, grains on the stroke-parallel strips slide back and forth and never reach a hole (see the test below). Options: centre the hole grid with margins no wider than a bar, or add some sideways motion to the sim stroke before reading 5 mm hold-ups as real.
2. **Flag marginal grains in `designs_holder.ideal_zone`.** Report grains whose smallest hole is within about 0.05–0.1 mm of an opening separately, as "marginal". At 2 mm that is a third of the brown rice, and their pass or hold is decided by print tolerance, contact softness and pegging, not by size.

## Open questions for the team

Measure into `bench/grain_dims.csv` (BENCH.md step 3): 30 grains of each kind at random, L, W and T with calipers, all 30 weighed together. In priority order:

1. **Read the packets.** What is the pale cream grain: couscous (fine or medium) or quinoa? A one-minute test: soak 10 grains for 5 minutes. Couscous swells and squashes under a fingernail, while quinoa, millet and amaranth stay hard. What is the white rice variety? What does the wild-rice blend contain: black japonica, a mahogany or red rice, Wehani, white rice? No long red Wehani grains are visible.
2. **black_rice thickness.** 1.95 mm is a literature value, and the photo hints at 2.5 for grains on edge. Against a 2 mm gap that decides pass or hold. Split black from red by colour when measuring. If they differ in size, make them two kinds.
3. **brown_rice width and thickness.** At 2.2 × 1.85 mm its smallest hole is 2.03 mm, right at the 2 mm screen, so a 0.1 mm error in the hole or the width moves a third of the grains. The thickness decides a 2 mm slot: the sources span 1.80–1.98 mm, which moves slot passage from 99% to 56%.
4. **wild_rice width and thickness, separately.** No measured thickness was found in the literature, and the widths disagree (1.2–1.5 mm for grade B [10], 2.5 mm in [4]). Also: what fraction is broken (the photo shows several at 40–60% length; grades allow 5–20% [10]), and are kernels bowed? A two-capsule bent kernel and a broken fraction are not built.
5. **couscous size, spread and thickness.** Measure 30 at random, including the small ones, and note fines. The 1.6 mm width is read from the photo against chia. The spread is the open part: [15]'s industrial couscous (σ/d50 ≈ 0.22) and [17]'s scan (CV 0.09) differ by more than 2×. At 22%, a 1.2 mm hole passes 99% of chia and 22% of couscous, so the two can't be cleanly sorted by size. At 9% they separate at about 1.2 mm. If the spread is much wider than 22%, split it into size classes.
6. **chia thickness** and a 100-seed weighing.
7. **Record the white rice as `white_rice` and the ditalini as `pasta_bench`** (Suggested changes 5). Ditalini: outer diameter, bore, length, and a weighing of 30.
8. **The irregular dark items, 4–6 mm.** One is Y-shaped, just outside the cup's upper-left rim, and 3–4 lumpy or spiky bits are in the clear box. Are they insect specimens planted as targets (`insects.py` material), broken grains, or debris?
9. **Non-grain bits in the clear box.** A red-and-white paper scrap about 5 × 9 mm, a pink bit, white paper-like bits and a grey translucent flake about 20 × 4 mm. Remove them before bench runs.
10. **Is the clear box a 12-well plate lid** (ANSI/SLAS footprint, 127.76 × 85.48 mm [21])? Its width and the ring pitch agree with the ditalini scale to within 1–3%. If so, it is a built-in scale bar.
11. **The round cup** (white, about 70 mm across) holds the same blend as the clear box, not just brown rice: brown rice, black and red ovals, 2–3 wild-rice needles and 5 ditalini, with about 40 grains spilled outside its left side. What is it for?
12. **A better photo.** Send the original camera file, shot straight down, with a steel ruler lying at grain level in each compartment. This one is a 1079 × 1452 screenshot, about 5 px/mm, so chia and couscous are only good to ±0.2 mm (±12–15%).
13. **Contacts for the small seeds.** MuJoCo's soft-contact overlap doesn't shrink with grain size. At the run below (dt 0.125 ms, time constant 0.25 ms) the worst overlap was 0.085 mm on the tray (0.036 mm in the first run). That is 10% of a chia's thickness, and chia and couscous gates would leave only 0.1–0.3 mm of clearance. `sorter.run` reports one overlap for the whole run, not one per kind, so which grain it was isn't known. Chickpea against chia is a 260:1 mass ratio. Should small-seed studies default to `contact_rule.py`'s stiffest setting, and report overlap per kind?
14. **White chia:** about 5% of the seeds. They are folded into `chia`. Do they need sorting separately?
15. **Not modelled:** wet chia mucilage, couscous swelling, clumping and fines, electrostatic cling of 1 mg seeds to printed plastic, and couscous's surface (a smooth ellipsoid may roll more than a real granule).

Rough counts in the photo, by eye or from area: pasta about 115, white rice 150–250, brown rice about 300, black and red rice about 130, wild rice about 30, couscous at least 600, chia at least 700. For a counted run (BENCH.md step 4), count them by hand.

## Code notes

- `SPREAD` holds one value, or a (length, width, thickness) tuple per kind. `CLIP` widens the ±20% clip for wild rice (±30%) and couscous (±40%). Every kind still takes exactly 3 normal draws per grain, so adding a new kind to a mix shifts the rng stream the same way any kind does.
- `SIZE_LINKED` kinds (couscous) read their `SPREAD` as (CV of L/W, CV of W, CV of T/W): the width factor multiplies all three dimensions, and the length and thickness factors are shape noise around it. `load_measured()` measures those three CVs for them.
- `volume(kind, dims)` is the shape volume `make()` builds: a capsule for wild rice, an ellipsoid otherwise. `load_measured()` uses it for density = mass / volume. For the new kinds it divides by the mean volume per grain, because with a size-linked spread the volume at the mean size is about 15% low. For the new kinds it stores a per-dimension spread. The frozen kinds keep the volume at the mean size and a single mean spread, unchanged.
- Wild rice is one straight capsule (`fromto` along x). MuJoCo computes its mass from the capsule volume, with no overlap correction needed. A bent two- or three-capsule kernel would need its density scaled down by the joint overlap (about 12% with 2 capsules, 24% with 3).
- The rice-family kinds and chia use the plain ellipsoid branch. Couscous is an ellipsoid envelope; a lumpy 3–4-sphere variant was not built.
